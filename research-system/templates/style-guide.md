# Style guide (default)

Copy this into a project's `00-brief/` and change anything that project needs differently.

## Voice and tone
- Professional, plain and direct. Write for a busy reader who is not a specialist.
- Lead with the conclusion, then the evidence.
- Use active voice. Prefer short sentences (aim for under 25 words).
- No hype or filler ("cutting-edge", "game-changing", "it is worth noting that").
- State uncertainty plainly: "Evidence is limited to one 2023 survey [3]."

## Language
- Spelling: British English <!-- change to US English if needed -->
- Define every acronym on first use: "Electric vehicle (EV)".
- Numbers: words for one to nine, digits for 10 and above; always digits with units and percentages (5%, 3 km).
- Dates: 14 October 2026. Currency: write the code or symbol, e.g. SGD 1,200 or $1,200 (USD).

## Structure
- Headings in sentence case ("Key findings", not "Key Findings").
- Numbered headings for reports over 1,500 words (1, 1.1, 1.2).
- Bullet lists for 3+ parallel items; keep bullets parallel in grammar.
- Every table and figure has a numbered caption and a source line.

## Citations
- Default style: numbered references in square brackets, e.g. "...grew 12% [3]."
- Reference list at the end, in order of first citation:
  `[3] Author/Organisation. (Year). Title. Publisher. URL (accessed DD Month YYYY).`
- Every fact, figure and quote needs a citation. No citation, no claim.

## Slides
- One idea per slide; the title states the takeaway ("Costs fell 30% since 2020").
- At most 5 bullets per slide, at most 12 words per bullet.
- Body text at least 18pt; titles at least 28pt.
- Final slide: sources.

## Spreadsheets
- One table per sheet, header row in bold, no merged cells.
- Units in column headers ("Cost (USD)"). Include a "Source" column.
