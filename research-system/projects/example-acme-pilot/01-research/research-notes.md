# Research notes: example-acme-pilot

## Research questions
1. How did output, customer service and absence change during the pilot (Jan to Jun 2026) compared with the six months before it (Jul to Dec 2025)?
2. How did staff wellbeing change, and did it differ between teams?
3. What problems came up during the pilot, and what caused them?

Scope: single UK site. Sources: only the three files in `00-brief/` ([1], [2], [3]).

**Working: totals and averages**

*Units shipped* [1]
- Before total: 10,120 + 9,870 + 10,340 + 10,410 + 10,650 + 9,480 = 60,870. Monthly average = 60,870 / 6 = 10,145.
- Pilot total: 9,910 + 10,230 + 10,580 + 10,390 + 10,720 + 10,600 = 62,430. Monthly average = 62,430 / 6 = 10,405.
- Change: +1,560 units in total (+260 per month), which is +2.6% (1,560 / 60,870 = 2.56%).

*On-time delivery %* [1] (simple, unweighted average of monthly figures)
- Before: (93.1 + 92.4 + 93.8 + 94.0 + 93.5 + 91.9) = 558.7. 558.7 / 6 = 93.12%.
- Pilot: (92.7 + 94.2 + 95.0 + 94.6 + 95.3 + 94.9) = 566.7. 566.7 / 6 = 94.45%.
- Change: +1.33 percentage points. I did not weight by volume, because order counts are not given.

*Customer complaints* [1]
- Before total: 14 + 17 + 12 + 15 + 13 + 19 = 90. Monthly average = 15.0.
- Pilot total: 16 + 11 + 10 + 12 + 9 + 18 = 76. Monthly average = 12.67.
- Change: -14 complaints, which is -15.6% (14 / 90).
- Complaints per 1,000 units: before 90 / 60.87 = 1.48. Pilot 76 / 62.43 = 1.22. That is -17.7%.

*Sick days (total, all staff)* [1]
- Before total: 61 + 58 + 66 + 72 + 79 + 84 = 420. Monthly average = 70.0.
- Pilot total: 52 + 47 + 44 + 40 + 38 + 41 = 262. Monthly average = 43.67.
- Change: -158 days, which is -37.6% (158 / 420).

**Working: per-head adjustment** (headcount rose from 48 to 51) [1]
- Headcount-months, before: 48 + 48 + 49 + 49 + 50 + 50 = 294. Average headcount = 49.0.
- Headcount-months, pilot: 50 + 50 + 51 + 51 + 51 + 51 = 304. Average headcount = 50.67.
- Units per head per month: before 60,870 / 294 = 207.0. Pilot 62,430 / 304 = 205.4. Change: -1.7 units, which is -0.8%.
- Sick days per head per month: before 420 / 294 = 1.43. Pilot 262 / 304 = 0.86. Change: -39.7%.
- Complaints per head per month: before 90 / 294 = 0.31. Pilot 76 / 304 = 0.25. Change: -18.3%.

**Findings**
- Total units shipped were 2.6% higher in the pilot (62,430 vs 60,870). [1] (single source)
- Per head, output was roughly flat to slightly lower (207.0 vs 205.4 units per head per month, -0.8%). The total rise is therefore partly due to the extra staff. [1] (single source)
- On-time delivery averaged 94.45% in the pilot vs 93.12% before (+1.33 points). The pilot's best month was May (95.3%) and the worst before-pilot month was December (91.9%). [1] (single source)
- Complaints fell from 90 to 76 (-15.6%). Per 1,000 units the fall is -17.7%. [1] (single source)
- Sick days fell from 420 to 262 (-37.6%). Per head the fall is -39.7%. [1] (single source)
- Absence rose steadily through the before period (61 in July to 84 in December) and fell steadily through the pilot (52 in January to 38 in May), then ticked up to 41 in June. [1] (single source)
- The June complaints figure (18) is the highest of the pilot and is close to the before-period peak (19 in December). It is 9 higher than May (9). [1] (single source)
- Sensitivity check: December 2025 was the worst before-pilot month on all four measures (units 9,480, on-time 91.9%, complaints 19, sick days 84). Excluding December, the before averages are:
  - Units: 51,390 / 5 = 10,278 per month.
  - On-time: 466.8 / 5 = 93.36%.
  - Complaints: 71 / 5 = 14.2 per month.
  - Sick days: 336 / 5 = 67.2 per month.
  - The pilot is still better than that baseline on every measure, but the gaps are smaller. Units are +127 per month (+1.2%) and on-time is +1.09 points. [1] (single source; derived calculation)
- The pilot shows output and on-time delivery at or above the before-period levels. The CSV has no team-level or Friday-specific operations data, so it cannot show where any change occurred. [1] (single source)

### Q2. Wellbeing and team differences

**Survey change (1 to 5 scale, 5 = best) [2]**

| Measure | Dec 2025 | Jun 2026 | Change (working) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overall job satisfaction | 3.2 | 3.9 | 3.9 - 3.2 = +0.7 |
| Burnout (5 = no burnout) | 2.7 | 3.6 | 3.6 - 2.7 = +0.9 |
| Work-life balance | 2.9 | 4.1 | 4.1 - 2.9 = +1.2 |
| Workload is manageable | 3.1 | 3.0 | 3.0 - 3.1 = -0.1 |
| Would recommend Acme as employer | 3.4 | 4.0 | 4.0 - 3.4 = +0.6 |

**Findings**
- Four of five measures improved. The largest gain was work-life balance (+1.2), then burnout (+0.9), satisfaction (+0.7) and employer recommendation (+0.6). [2] (single source)
- "Workload is manageable" did not improve (3.1 to 3.0, -0.1). The summary does not say whether this change is meaningful. [2] (single source)
- Response rates: December 2025 was 41 of 50 (41 / 50 = 82%). June 2026 was 37 of 51 (37 / 51 = 72.5%, reported as 73%). [2] (single source)
- Team results (overall satisfaction, June 2026 only): Production 4.1 (n=19), Warehouse 4.0 (n=10), Customer service 3.1 (n=8). [2] (single source)
- Customer service was 0.8 to 1.0 points below the other two teams (4.1 - 3.1 = 1.0; 4.0 - 3.1 = 0.9). [2] (single source)
- Consistency check: the team n values sum to 19 + 10 + 8 = 37, which matches the June respondents. The weighted mean is (4.1 x 19 + 4.0 x 10 + 3.1 x 8) / 37 = 142.7 / 37 = 3.86, which rounds to the reported 3.9. [2] (derived check)
- Free-text comments: the most common theme (14 comments) was "more time with family / for rest". The second (6 comments, 5 from customer service) was that Friday cover was stressful and customers waited longer for replies. [2] (single source)
- The manager's log independently says production and warehouse adapted well and customer service is the weak point. This is consistent with the team survey pattern. [2][3] (confirmed: 2 sources, but both are internal and not independent of each other in method)

### Q3. Problems and causes

**Findings (all from the manager's log [3] unless stated)**
- January: a two-week adjustment period. Production planning moved to a Monday to Thursday schedule, and output dipped slightly while shifts were re-planned. [3] This is consistent with [1], where January had the lowest units of the pilot (9,910). However, January was still higher than December (9,480) and below the before-period average (10,145). [1][3] (confirmed: 2 sources)
- February: meetings were cut from 30 to 15 minutes by default, and the weekly all-hands moved to a written update. The log records this as a change made, not as a problem. [3] (single source)
- March: no major issues. [3] (single source; in [1] March has the highest on-time figure before May, at 95.0%, and complaints of 10)
- April: customer service raised concerns. Friday cover is one person on a rota, phone queue times rose, and no extra hire was made. [3] (single source)
- June: complaints spiked to 18. Most related to slow Friday responses during a product recall notice that went out on a Thursday. The operations manager judged it mostly a one-off but linked it to thin Friday cover. [3] The 18 figure is confirmed in [1]. The cause is the manager's judgement only. (single source for cause)
- Friday cover is the recurring cause named across sources. The survey comments (6 comments, 5 from customer service, about stressful Friday cover and longer waits) match the April and June entries in the log. [2][3] (confirmed: 2 sources)
- The log's overall view is that production and warehouse adapted well, and that Friday cover should be fixed before any permanent change. This is the manager's recommendation, recorded here as a source statement only. [3] (single source)
- The log says no extra hire was made. Headcount rose from 50 to 51 in March [1]. The files do not say which role that hire filled. [1][3]

## Conflicting evidence
- No direct factual conflicts between the three sources.
- Tension: overall indicators improved ([1], [2]), but customer service staff report lower satisfaction (3.1) and Friday-cover stress ([2][3]). The April and June entries, and June's complaint spike in [1], fit this. Total complaints still fell over the six months.
- Tension: total output rose 2.6%, but per-head output fell 0.8%. Which figure matters depends on the question being asked. Both are shown above.
- "Slight dip in output" in January [3] is relative. January was above December but below the before-period average [1].

## Gaps and uncertainties
- [EVIDENCE GAP: no control group. The data cannot separate the effect of the four-day week from other changes during the same period, such as the hire, shorter meetings and the Monday to Thursday re-planning.]
- [EVIDENCE GAP: no seasonality baseline. Only one before period (Jul to Dec 2025) is provided, and no January to June 2025 data. Seasonal effects on output, absence and complaints cannot be assessed. The before and pilot windows cover different parts of the year.]
- [EVIDENCE GAP: December 2025 was the weakest month on all four operational measures. It lowers the before-period averages, which flatters the pilot comparison. The reason is not explained in any file. See the sensitivity check in Q1.]
- [EVIDENCE GAP: June 2026 complaint spike (18). The manager attributes it to a one-off recall notice plus thin Friday cover. There is no complaint-level data to verify this, and one month cannot show whether it is a trend. The CSV has no July data or later.]
- [EVIDENCE GAP: no pre-pilot team-level survey. Team satisfaction exists for June 2026 only. It cannot be shown whether customer service was already lower before the pilot, or whether it fell. The December overall figure (3.2) cannot be split by team.]
- [EVIDENCE GAP: the survey is anonymous, so individual changes cannot be tracked. Before and after respondents may be different people.]
- Response rates differ (82% vs 73%). The people who did not respond (9 of 50 in December, 14 of 51 in June) may differ from those who did. Non-response bias cannot be assessed. [2]
- Small samples: customer service n=8 and warehouse n=10 in June. Team averages could move substantially with a few responses. Team sizes (not just respondents) are not given. [2]
- [EVIDENCE GAP: no phone queue time or response time metric. April's "queue times rose" [3] and the survey comments [2] cannot be quantified. There is also no Friday-specific complaint or output data.]
- [EVIDENCE GAP: no team-level operations data (production, warehouse, customer service) in [1]. The claim that production and warehouse "adapted well" [3] cannot be checked against numbers.]
- [EVIDENCE GAP: no cost, revenue, staff turnover or hours-worked data. Pay and working-hour arrangements under the pilot are not described, so productivity per hour worked cannot be computed.]
- Only six months in each period. Pilot effects such as novelty or longer-term fatigue cannot be assessed.
- Definitions are not stated: how on-time % and sick days are measured, whether sick days count working days, and whether the four-day week changed the number of working days available. The sick-day totals may not be strictly comparable if working days per month differ. [1]
- On-time % averages are unweighted by volume. Weighted figures need order counts, which are not provided. [1]
- All data is fictional test data. [1][2][3]
- The manager's log is one person's account and is not dated per entry. [3]
