# Slides outline: four-day week pilot

## Slide 1. Four-day week pilot: extend with conditions
- Report to Acme's board of directors
- Pilot: January to June 2026
- Compared with July to December 2025
- All data is fictional test data

**Speaker note:** Open with the conclusion. We recommend extending the pilot, with conditions, rather than making it permanent now or ending it. All figures are fictional test data. The next slides give the evidence, the limits and the decision test.

## Slide 2. Sick days fell 37.6% and output held
- Sick days: 420 down to 262 [1]
- Complaints: 90 down to 76 [1]
- On-time delivery: 93.12% up to 94.45% [1]
- Total units up 2.6%; per head down 0.8% [1]

**Speaker note:** Absence is the strongest result. Total output rose, but headcount rose from 48 to 51, so output per person was flat to slightly lower. Read this as "output was maintained", not "output improved". On-time delivery is an unweighted average of monthly figures.

## Slide 3. Wellbeing improved, except in customer service
- Work-life balance: 2.9 up to 4.1 [2]
- Burnout score: 2.7 up to 3.6 [2]
- Job satisfaction: 3.2 up to 3.9 [2]
- Customer service satisfaction: 3.1, against 4.1 and 4.0 [2]
- Workload manageable: 3.1 to 3.0, no gain [2]

**Speaker note:** Scores run from one to five, and five is best. Four of five measures improved. The team breakdown exists for June 2026 only, and customer service had eight respondents. We cannot tell whether customer service was already lower before the pilot.

## Slide 4. Friday customer-service cover is the recurring problem
- One person covers Fridays; no extra hire made [3]
- Phone queue times rose in April [3]
- June complaints reached 18, up from nine [1]
- Five of six Friday-cover comments came from customer service [2]

**Speaker note:** The manager linked June mostly to a Thursday recall notice plus thin Friday cover. That cause is one person's judgement, and no queue-time data exists. Two internal sources agree on the weakness, so we treat it as real but cannot size it.

## Slide 5. Six months without a control group is thin evidence
- No control group; other changes happened too [1][3]
- No earlier-year data to check seasonality [1]
- December 2025 was weak, which flatters the comparison [1]
- Survey: summary only, response rates 82% and 73% [2]
- No data after June, or on cost [1]

**Speaker note:** Excluding December, units rose 1.2% and on-time delivery gained 1.09 points. Sick days still fell, from 67.2 to 43.67 a month. So the absence result survives the check, and the output result is weaker. Confidence overall is medium.

## Slide 6. Extend the pilot for at least six months, with conditions
- Fix Friday customer-service cover first
- Plan for urgent Friday notices
- Collect weekday complaints, response times and team surveys
- Fall-back: redesign or end, never permanent by default
- Test: complaints at or below 12.67 a month [1]

**Speaker note:** This is a judgement, not a result in the data. Confidence is medium. The decision test for permanency also includes: no month at 18 complaints, sick days well below 70 a month, and customer service satisfaction rising towards 4.0. The board sets the exact thresholds and may choose a different extension length.
